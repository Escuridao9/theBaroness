OUR STORY

A hundred years under one title.

The building at 248 Greenwich Street opened in the early 1920s, during Prohibition, when a name on the deed was a liability rather than an asset. Ownership was structured accordingly: the license was held not by a person but by a title, "the Baroness," passed from one steward to the next along with the ledger and the key. No name has ever replaced it.

That same need for discretion shaped the building itself. The cellar was built out for extraction and storage that had no reason to be visible from the street, and the private rooms were scattered rather than grouped — one behind the hearth, one behind drawn drapes, one reached by its own stair — so a meeting held in any of them left no trace on the café floor. When Prohibition ended, the need for secrecy didn't — the layout simply found new uses. The cellar now ages the coffee, spirits, wine, and beer on the current menu.

What survives from that period isn't really the building — it's the arrangement. A business with no listed proprietor, run by whoever inherits the title, largely unchanged for a century. The lantern above the door, lit without interruption since the year it opened, is the one part of the original design nobody has ever proposed doing away with.
